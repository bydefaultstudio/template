# Project management

## Before accepting a brief

Answer these, or list them as questions for Erlen:

- Business objective and how we'll measure success.
- Who it's for and what problem it solves for them.
- What happens if we do nothing. What the alternatives are.
- Why us, why now.
- Deliverables, deadlines, budget, approvers.

Weak answers to watch for: "a competitor launched one", "the CEO wants it", "we need a refresh".

## Writing a brief from rough notes

When Erlen or the team share notes, bullets, links or a client email:

1. Read everything given.
2. Draft the brief in the project's brief template format: project title, overview, objectives, target audience, deliverables, dates and timeline, stakeholders, constraints, open questions.
3. Fill every section or mark it **TBD**. Never invent details.
4. If anything important is unclear, ask **3 to 6 short, specific questions, most important first**, rather than guessing.
5. When answers come back, update the brief and return a final, copy-ready version.

**Brief only mode:** if someone just asks for a brief ("turn this into a brief"), produce the brief and stop. Don't create tasks, folders or Notion entries unless asked.

Get Erlen's sign-off on the brief before any work starts.

## Scoping: fixed time, flexible scope

- Set the **appetite** first: how much time is this worth? (Small: 1 to 2 weeks. Big: up to 6 weeks.)
- A pitch for work has: problem, appetite, rough solution, risks, and what's explicitly out.
- If work doesn't fit the appetite, cut scope rather than extending time. Raise it with Erlen.

## Review gates

| Gate | Checks |
|---|---|
| 1. Concept | Problem defined, several routes explored, fits the strategy |
| 2. Design | On brand, uses the system, responsive, real content |
| 3. Pre-handoff | Every state designed, edge cases, accessibility, specs, developer walkthrough |
| 4. Build QA | Built work matches the design |

Scale gates to the risk of the project. Each gate is logged in the project's log.

## Decisions

Before any decision, be clear on: what's being decided, by when, who decides, who is consulted, who can veto, who is told. Record every decision in the project's decision record with the reason and what it replaces. For choices between options, also record why each loser lost.

## Project state

Use these statuses for every task: `pending`, `in progress`, `waiting on evidence`, `done`, `deferred: reason`, `skipped: reason`. Gates can be deferred with a reason, never silently skipped.

## Negotiation scripts (drafts for Erlen, never sent by agents)

- **Deadline squeezed:** "We can deliver X by [date] if we drop Y and accept risk Z. Or we can phase it: A now, B by [date]."
- **Stakeholder overrides a decision** ("make the logo bigger"): ask what outcome they want, offer three ways to get it, record who decided.
- **Scope creep:** show the new request against the agreed scope and the appetite, with the cost in time.
- Always have a fallback position. Confirm agreements in writing.

## Workshops and sprints

- **Design sprint (5 days):** Mon map the problem and pick a target. Tue sketch (notes, ideas, Crazy 8s, solution sketch). Wed decide (gallery, heat map, vote, decider), storyboard. Thu prototype. Fri test with 5 users.
  Needs a decider in the room and real users booked two weeks ahead (book 6). A 4-day version works.
- Facilitation: don't let the most senior person facilitate. Draw out quiet people. Time-box the talkers.
- For positioning workshops, see `/bd:strategy`.

## Presentations

Structure: hook, context, journey, solution, evidence, the ask. For senior clients, lead with impact. Variants: work-in-progress update, design review, final presentation.

## Check-ins

Each check-in is under 10 lines: due today, late, waiting on Erlen, done since last check-in. Only report numbers you actually counted.

> Not covered by any reviewed repo: statements of work, estimating, change requests, limits on feedback rounds, and client approval flows. These need writing with Erlen and Syeda from how By Default actually works.
