# Branding

## Every client gets a brand kit

Keep three files in the client's brand folder (in agency: `clients/<client>/brand/`). Read them before any work for that client.

- `mood.md`: personality words, references, do's and don'ts.
- `voice.md`: tone, vocabulary, words to avoid, with examples and counter-examples.
- `tokens.md`: colours, type, spacing, radius, motion values, logo rules.

If a file is missing, say so and skip that check. **Never invent brand rules.**

## Working with an existing brand

- Keep its palette, type and logo rules. Take only composition and craft from elsewhere.
- Use official logo files with their clear-space rules. Never recolour or redraw a mark.
- Match motion personality to the brand: playful can bounce, premium should be crisp.

## New identity: process

1. **Strategy first.** Positioning and the idea come before visuals (see `/bd:strategy`).
2. **Subject inventory and idea sentence** (see `/bd:design-direction`).
3. **Routes.** Three genuinely different routes to Erlen, each built from a different premise. Round one is type-led. For each: idea, signature element, colour world, type roles, what it rejects.
4. **Test each route** across real applications, not just the logo: a website hero, a social post, signage or packaging, a small favicon size, and black and white.
5. **Refine one route.** Record why the others lost and what would revive them.
6. **Guidelines.** Logo (construction, clear space, minimum sizes, misuse), colour (values for print and screen, proportions), type (roles, scale, licensing), imagery, motion, voice, and applications.

> Note: none of the reviewed skill repos covered identity craft (logos, marks, systems, guidelines) in depth. Steps 4 to 6 are a By Default draft for Erlen to refine.

## Verbal identity

- **Voice stays constant; tone flexes by situation.** Document both with examples.
- Tone by moment:

| Moment | Tone |
|---|---|
| Loading or waiting | Calm, present |
| Empty state | Inviting, one next step |
| User error | Blame-free, how to fix it |
| Our error | Own it, plainly |
| Success | Warm and brief |

- **One-liner test:** "We help [who] who struggle with [problem] to [solution] so they can [result]." Someone should be able to repeat it after hearing it once. Use as structure, not as the brand's actual voice.

## Concepts and campaigns

Check any concept or headline against **SUCCESs**: Simple (one core idea), Unexpected (breaks a pattern), Concrete (real details, real numbers), Credible (provable), Emotional (one person, not a crowd), Story.

For work meant to spread, check **STEPPS**: Social currency, Triggers, high-arousal Emotion, Public visibility, Practical value, Stories.

## Turning a feeling into design

1. Name the feeling the brand should create.
2. Find a physical equivalent (a heavy door, a snap, a slow pour).
3. Pull out its behaviour: weight, speed, recovery.
4. Map that to easing, copy, colour temperature and spacing.

Test: strip out the colour and imagery. Does the feeling survive?

## Brand compliance check

Review against `mood.md`, `voice.md` and `tokens.md`. List every hard-coded value that should be a token, every off-voice line, and every off-mood choice. Use the `/bd:design-critique` format.
