# Critique

## How to look

1. **Experience it cold first.** No guided tour. Real content where possible.
2. **Review against the promise.** What was this meant to do (from the brief)? The gap between promise and result is the review.
3. **Check through these lenses, in this order:** idea, hierarchy, composition, typography, imagery, colour, detail, motion. Then accessibility and brand compliance.
4. **Screenshots at 1440, 768 and 390px** for anything on the web.

## How to write each point

| Part | What it says |
|---|---|
| **Observation** | What's there, neutrally |
| **Problem** | Why it matters |
| **Fix** | The exact change (value, token, ratio, move) |
| **Rating** | Blocker / Should fix / Note |

- Lead with the biggest issue. No compliment sandwich.
- Separate taste ("I'd push the scale further") from defects ("fails contrast at 3.1:1").
- Drop findings that are just "a bold choice working as intended" or anything already agreed in the project's decision record.
- Write as one designer to a colleague.

## Score (for comparisons and tracking)

| Area | Weight |
|---|---|
| Typography | 25% |
| Composition | 25% |
| Motion and interaction | 20% |
| Colour | 15% |
| Details and craft | 15% |

**Caps:** if the work "could belong to anyone", it can't score above 6/10, however polished. Gradient-plus-eyebrow-plus-three-cards template work caps at 6.5.

## Verdict

Binary: **"Great"** or **"Not done yet"**, with a ranked fix list and a cut list (what to remove). Three rounds of review at most before escalating to Erlen.

## Running a critique session with people

- The designer says what stage it's at and what feedback they want.
- Present 5 min, clarifying questions 5 min, structured feedback, discussion, then actions with owners and dates.
- Useful openers: "I notice…", "I wonder…", "What if…", "I think… because…".
- Exploration crits and refinement crits are different meetings.
