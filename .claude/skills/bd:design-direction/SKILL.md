# Finding a direction

The single biggest risk in AI design work is the median answer: the look a similar prompt would give anyone. This skill is how we avoid it.

## 1. Inventory the subject (before any visuals)

List 8 to 12 things only this client or project has. Look in:

- the name and what it means
- place: street, city, venue, region
- materials, objects, products, tools
- rituals, routines, rules, constraints
- printed and physical artefacts (tickets, packaging, signage, uniforms)
- numbers that matter to them
- the culture and community around them

If the brief gives no subject, propose one and ask Erlen to confirm.

## 2. Write the idea sentence

> On this [page / identity / campaign], [subject thing] becomes [device].

Devices can come from the subject's output, an object, the name, a place, a ritual, an artefact, or its opposite.

**Competitor test:** swap in the nearest rival's name. If the sentence still works, it's a category idea, not a client idea. Rewrite it.

## 3. Generate genuinely different routes

- Derive each route from a different premise in the brief's evidence. Never from a style menu ("Brutalist", "Y2K") and never by rolling dice.
- Routes must differ in idea, structure and reading path, not just colour and font. If swapping the fonts or palette makes two routes the same, rebuild one.
- First round is type-led with real or placeholder copy. Defer production details.
- Present **three routes to Erlen**, each with: the idea sentence, one signature element, colour world, type roles, and the defaults it rejects.

## 4. Plan, check, then build

Before making anything, write a short plan: 4 to 6 named colours (names that evoke the subject, like `--kerb-grey`, not `--gray-700`), type roles, layout in a few lines, and the one signature moment people will screenshot.

Then ask: **would a similar brief from another studio land here?** If yes, revise and say what changed.

## 5. Spend boldness in one place

One memorable element. Everything else supports it. Before handing over, remove one accessory.

## Defaults to avoid unless the brief or brand asks for them

**Colour:** SaaS blue (#2563EB, #3B82F6), purple-to-blue gradients, cream with terracotta and a serif, near-black with one acid accent, beige "warmth".

**Type:** Inter, Roboto, Poppins, Space Grotesk, DM Sans or Playfair + Inter as the lead face. Also avoid swapping in a new fixed favourite; that's sameness by another name.

**Layout:** hero, logo strip, three feature cards, testimonial, CTA. Every section fading up on scroll. Every element centred. A distinctive hero followed by stock blocks.

**Decoration by default:** glassmorphism, blob backgrounds, grain, gradient mesh, custom cursors, noise, device frames, icon circles.

**Template chrome:** tracked all-caps eyebrows above every heading, one accented word in a headline, "→" on every button, meta strings like "A · B · C", fake dashboards made of grey bars.

**Copy:** "Get started", "Learn more", invented testimonials, logos or stats.

## When a "default" is right

Streetwear, editorial and music work legitimately use mono labels, index numbers, hairline rules and condensed caps. Use them when the concept calls for them, not as decoration. If a client's existing brand owns a device on this list, the brand wins. Record the exception in the project's decision record.

Erlen can override any rule here. Log the override and the reason.
